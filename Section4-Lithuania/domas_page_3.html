Šaltibarščiai

Šaltibarščiai (cold beetroot soup) is a must-try when visiting Lithuania. It is a truly unique dish, consisting of grated beetroot (or pickeld beetroot), kefir (fermented milk), eggs, and other refreshing ingredients such as diced cucumber and dill. It typically comes served with boiled potatoes on the side to add flavour. What makes the dish even more special is its eye-catching distinct pink coloration which it gets from the beetroots. You may doubt the fact that it's made from kefir, but trust me, you wont regret trying it!

Suggestion:

Since cold beet soup is, well, cold, it would primarily be the perfect meal on a hot summer day (though you shouldn't be shoo-d off from trying it only and only if its hot!). The refreshing properties of cold beet soup will surely leave you more than satisfied!

  1. List of ingredients

    • 4 large eggs
    • 1 quart of kefir
    • 500g of beetroots
    • 1 large cucumber
    • fresh dill
    • fresh chives
    • salt and pepper to taste
    • A few medium potatoes

  2. Preparation

    • Preparing the ingredients
      1. Take the large eggs, and hard boil them. Once they've cooled, dice them up.
      2. Take the potatoes, peel them and dice them into small chunks. Bring some salted water to a boil and let the potatoes boil until they're soft, then drain the water from the potatoes.
      3. peel and shred the beetroots using a grater
      4. Quarter and dice the cucumber
      5. Chop up the chives and some of the dill.

  3. Making the dish

    • Putting it all together
      1. Take a large pot, and add all of the prepared ingredients apart from the potatoes. Season to taste with salt and pepper, and stir well. Put in a refirgerator to cool before serving. Sprinkle some chopped chives and dill onto the soup and serve with hot, boiled potatoes.

  4. Cultural Importance

    Šaltibarščiai stands the test of time since its first appearance in the 17th century, being a core part of Lithuanian cuisine. It's vibrant pink color and distinct yet refreshing flavour has united many Eastern-European countries together through its taste, and has spread outside of Europe to be experienced by the world!
