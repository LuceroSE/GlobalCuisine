Bulviniai Blynai (Potato Pancakes)

Potato pancakes are common around all of Lithuania and are as easy to make as they seem. They are, at the core, made of grated potato, but to add some extra flavour, diced onion, grated carrots, and egg are added. In many cases, people like to add meat in the center of the pancake to give them a kick in the flavour department. This recipe will show you how to make the non-meat version.

Interesting fact about potato pancakes:

Culturally speaking, it is fairly common (if not extremely common,) to find Lithuanians eating potato pancakes with sourcream (in fact, many of our dishes end up being eaten with sourcream added somewhere in the mix). Try it for yourselves and see what you think of it!

  1. List of ingredients

    • 6 medium-sized potatoes
    • 1 egg
    • 1 onion
    • salt
    • pepper
    • oil

  2. Preparation

    • Batter preparation
      • Take your potatoes, peel them and wash them. Then use a grater to grate them, and put them in a bowl, draining any excess fluid if needed. Then take your onion, peel it, dice it, and put it in the same bowl. Next, crack an egg into the bowl and then add flour (to give the batter some structure). Lastly, add salt and pepper to taste and mix the batter well.

  3. Cooking

      1. Take a pan and add some oil to it (enough to be able to fry the batter well), and heat the oil.
      2. Add some batter to the pan using a large spoon (pancake size depends on individual's preference)
      3. Let the batter fry until it is golden brown in color, then flip it and let the other side fry until it is also golden brown.
      4. Once the pancakes are cooked, you can season them with green onion and or sour cream (if you wish).
      5. Enjoy!

  4. Cultural Importance

    In the 17th century, one of the richest Lithuanian families began growing potatoes on their land, and so, decided to put them to good use by making potato pancakes. Soon after, the poor began cooking these too, up-bringing its popularity due to its low cost. Over time, this became a common dish among the poor and due to this, it is still popular today.
