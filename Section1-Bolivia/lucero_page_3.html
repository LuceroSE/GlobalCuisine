Charquekan

Charquekan is the typical dish of Oruro, a city located in the west of Bolivia. Known as one of the coldest cities in the country, Oruro is famous among Bolivians for its desert-like landscapes, where hundreds of llamas often graze. While llamas are widely considered adorable animals, Bolivians also view them as an important source of livestock. Charquekan can be made with beef, but it is most traditionally made with llama meat. To prepare this dish, the llama meat must be sun-dried and cured into jerky.

Fun fact about Charquekan!

While eating llama meat may be shocking to some foreigners, it is actually known to be one of the healthiest meats. Llama meat is rich in lean, low-fat protein and contains significantly less cholesterol than regular beef. In the past, it was considered a food for poorer communities, but as people began to understand its health benefits, its popularity grew. Today, llama meat is highly valued and is considered just as important as beef in many regions of South America

  1. List of ingredients

    • 1/2 kg sun-dried llama meat (or substitute with beef jerky)
    • 1 kg corn (preferably white corn, choclo)
    • 2 eggs
    • 1/4 kg soft cheese of your choice (preferably fresh cheese but mozzarella also works)
    • 4 large potatoes
    • 2 fresh chillies (locotos or any medium-spicy variety, this is for the llajwa)
    • 5 medium tomatos
    • Salt to taste
    • 1 cup oil (divided for frying and other uses)
    • 1 lemon

  2. Pre-preparations and tips

    • Prepare the Meat:
      • Slice the meat very thinly and generously sprinkle both sides with salt. Place the meat on a tray and leave it in a sunny area for a couple of days, in case you cannot find sun-dried llama meat at the market.
    • Shred the Dried Meat
      • Shred the jerky (dried meat or charque) into flaky pieces and store it in a container for when you're ready to fry it.
    • Prepare the Potatoes
      • Thoroughly wash the potatoes and peel them if desired

  3. Preparation

    • Cooking the corn
      • Cook the corn (choclo) in a pot with water until soft. This can take around 20-30 minutes depending on the variety Once cooked, drain the water and allow it to cool. It can be stored in the fridge if preparing in advance.
    • Cooking the potatoes
      • Boil the potatoes in salted water until tender, then set them aside. If you prefer crispy potatoes, fry them in oil after boiling until golden brown (but charquekan it is traditionally eaten with boiled potatoes).
    • Prepare the llajwa
      • Blend the chillies and tomatoes until the sauce reaches your preferred consistency (smooth or slightly chunky). Add salt and a squeeze of lemon juice to taste. (llajwa is prepared in different ways but this is the base of a good llajwa). Store the sauce in a small dish and refrigerate until needed. You can deseed the chillies if you prefer the sauce mildly spicy.
    • Boil the eggs
      • Place the eggs in a pot of water and boil for 10 minutes until fully cooked. Once cooled, peel and halve the eggs.

  4. Cooking and Plating the dish

    • Frying the Meat
      1. Set a frying pan with oil at medium heat. Once the oil is hot add the shredded dried llama meat and fry until crispy, stirring occasionally. Take care not to let the meat burn, as llama meat cooks quickly.
    • Plating the Dish
      1. Start with a portion of corn as the base of the plate.
      2. Place the hard-boiled eggs next to the corn.
      3. Add the prepared potatoes to the plate.
      4. Top the potatoes with the crispy llama meat
      5. Finish by adding a slice of cheese on top.
      6. Serve the llajwa on the side or drizzle it over the dish as desired.

  5. Cultural Importance

    Charquekan is more than just a dish, it’s a key part of Bolivia’s culture, especially in the west. It reflects the Andean lifestyle, where people had to find smart ways to preserve food in the very high-altitudes, as Oruro sits at 3700 metres above sea level. Charquekan has become a dish that brings people together and it is often served during festivals and family gatherings. Charquekan is a reminder of Bolivia’s agricultural traditions.
