The Giant of Africa

Nigeria is known for its diversity of people and languages, its vast expanse of land and its population (the biggest in Africa). Nigeria borders Niger, Chad, Cameroon, Benin and the Gulf of Guinea, which borders the Atlantic Ocean.

Nigerian cuisine is similar to other West African cuisines, as it is known for being savoury and spicy. Each Nigerian dish can have incredible versitility due to there being so many different ethnic groups, each having their own spin on a dish. Nigeria's food mainly uses spices and herbs with palm oil or groundnut oil to create deeply flavored sauces and soups.

The baseline of many Nigerian dishes is made up of ingredients like rice, vegetables, locust beans, onions, peppers, groundnuts, biscuit bones and several types of meat. Nigerian cuisine uses several spices but the ones used in most dishes are Iru, Uda and Ogiri. Among its most famous meals are Jollof Rice, Pounded Yam, and Egusi stew. With over 300 traditional dishes, Nigeria has plenty of enriching foods to offer to your culinary experience.

On this page, you’ll discover the recipe for a Nigerian starter/appetizer for parties, Nigerias most cherished meal, a meal guaranteed to give you lots of protein and a tasty snack! We invite you to explore these culinary treasures and immerse yourself in this informative experience.
